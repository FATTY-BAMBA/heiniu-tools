CHAPTER 00

Why most people barely use Claude's potential

Move beyond one-off questions: give context, organize recurring work, and ask for useful results.

Most people ask a quick question, copy the answer into another app, and start over next time. Instead, give Claude useful background, use Projects for repeat work, connect relevant sources when appropriate, and request finished outputs.

Try contrasting “Write an apology email” with “In my customer-support project, reply to this complaint using our store policy and warm tone.” For travel, provide your group size, Taiwan destination, budget, and preferences rather than asking for a generic list.

CHAPTER 01

Plans and models: what do you need?

Start free, then upgrade only when your actual workflow justifies it.

Claude offers different usage tiers and model options. A lightweight model is useful for simple formatting and classification; an everyday model can handle most writing and analysis; a more capable model may help with complex reasoning. Plan names, pricing, availability, and usage limits can change, so check official Claude pricing before paying.

Common pitfalls: choosing the most expensive tier before you use it regularly, using the heaviest model for every small task, and keeping unrelated work in one overly long chat.

CHAPTER 02

Memory: help Claude understand your context

Share relevant preferences once—and review what gets remembered.

Memory can help Claude retain useful context about your work and communication style. Check the current memory controls in settings. Only share information you are comfortable storing; never import passwords, identification numbers, or private account credentials.

To move context from another chatbot, ask for a summary, edit it carefully, and use Claude's import option if it appears in your account. Afterward, ask Claude to summarize what it learned and correct any errors.

CHAPTER 03

Prompting: context, task, format

The easiest prompt improvement is to write a clearer brief.

Treat Claude like a talented new coworker. Explain the situation, state one specific task, and describe your desired format. “Write a shipping-delay message” is vague. “Our longtime customer’s jacket is five days late; write a friendly LINE reply under 150 words and offer a shipping voucher” gives it something useful to work with.

Two tips: for complicated tasks, ask Claude three clarification questions before starting; and paste an example of writing you like instead of describing a tone in abstract terms.

Reusable three-part prompt

Reusable three-part prompt
Context: [Who I am, background, and intended audience]
Task: [Exactly what I need done]
Format: [Length, tone, bullets or table, number of versions]
Here is a good example for style: [paste example]
Before starting, ask me three questions if anything essential is missing.

CHAPTER 04

Projects: one workspace for each recurring job

Set context and rules once for work you do repeatedly.

Create Projects for customer service, quotes, job applications, family travel, or personal budgeting. Each should have its own relevant reference files and working instructions. In Claude, create a Project, attach the references you need, add guidelines, and test with a realistic task.

Keep Projects focused and current. If a price list or policy changes, replace the old file. Avoid mixing unrelated tasks or assuming a Project can access documents you never provided.

Customer support Project instructions

Customer support Project instructions
You are the support assistant for [store].
Use our attached return policy and FAQ.
When I paste a LINE or Instagram message:
1. Acknowledge the customer warmly.
2. Give a concise reply under 100 words.
3. Do not invent policy exceptions.
4. Flag any situation that needs my decision.
5. Draft only; never send without approval.

CHAPTER 05

Connect Claude to your tools

Use trusted data sources instead of endless copying and pasting.

Check Claude's connector settings for available integrations such as Gmail, calendars, cloud storage, and Slack. Availability depends on your account and organization. Connect only what you need, review permissions, and start with a read-only question.

For Taiwan-specific platforms without supported connectors, export a CSV or selectively paste information after removing sensitive fields. Always confirm drafts before allowing tools to send emails, change calendars, or delete content.

Calendar and Gmail

Drive document comparison

Personal bills and availability

Personal bills and availability

CHAPTER 06

Skills: teach Claude a repeatable process

A Project provides background. A Skill describes how to do the work.

Skills can package reusable instructions, templates, and sometimes executable resources. A customer-support Skill might classify an issue, check a return policy, draft a reply, and flag anything requiring a manager's decision. Explore the Heiniu AI Video Editor Skill for a practical example.

Before making a Skill, manually perform the process a few times, document each step and its quality checks, and test the reusable version. Install Skills only from sources you trust.

Create a reusable Skill

Create a reusable Skill
I do [repeat task] every week.
My steps are:
1. [First step]
2. [Second step]
3. [Third step]
I judge quality by [checklist].
Help me turn this into a reusable Skill. Ask any questions first, then draft instructions and test cases.

CHAPTER 07

Ask for deliverables, not only text

If you need a document, spreadsheet, or presentation, ask for the actual result.

Claude may be able to create downloadable files or interactive outputs depending on your plan and enabled features. Define the audience, content, structure, and file format. For example, ask for a five-slide management presentation with decisions, owners, deadlines, and next steps rather than loose paragraphs.

For spreadsheets, request formulas and test them. For Taiwan travel itineraries, specify the dates, transport, group size, budget in NT$, and whether each cost is estimated. Review every generated file before sharing.

Presentation

Presentation
Turn my attached meeting notes into a downloadable five-slide presentation if file creation is supported. Slide 1: key conclusion. Slides 2–4: decisions and responsible owners. Slide 5: next week's actions. Never invent missing facts.

Quote spreadsheet

Quote spreadsheet
Create an editable quote spreadsheet with item, quantity, unit price, subtotal, applicable 5% Taiwan business tax, and total. Include three sample rows and working formulas.

Tainan itinerary

Tainan itinerary
Create an editable two-day Tainan itinerary with date, time, venue, transport, and cost. We are two adults and two children, driving, avoiding spicy food, with a NT$10,000 budget. Show estimated totals and flag assumptions.

CHAPTER 08

Claude Code: make one useful tool

Start small, test thoroughly, and keep control of what code changes.

Claude Code can help build and debug software. Describe a focused problem such as a Taiwan take-home-pay calculator, request a mobile-friendly interface, and tell it to document the assumptions behind any insurance or tax rate.

Ask for at least three test cases including an edge case. Read the result and review commands before approving execution or deletion. A small working tool is better than a huge unfinished platform.

CHAPTER 09

Scheduled tasks: automate a recurring workflow

Run a task successfully once before scheduling it.

When scheduled tasks are available, you can request recurring briefings or reports. Examples include an 8 AM weekday calendar-and-email summary, a Monday Taiwan industry-news scan, a Friday work report, or Sunday family schedule planning.

Confirm the recurrence, time zone, permissions, and whether connected sources are available. Don't assume tasks requiring local files or approvals can finish unattended. Keep your schedule small enough that you actually read the results.

8 AM briefing

8 AM briefing
Every weekday at 8 AM, prepare a three-minute briefing of my connected calendar, customer emails needing replies, and three priorities. Provide source references and do not send emails.

Monday Taiwan industry scan

Monday Taiwan industry scan
Every Monday at 9 AM, find the past week's important Taiwan [industry] news and competitor updates. Summarize five developments with source links and possible business impact.

Friday status report

Friday status report
Every Friday at 4 PM, review this week's updated files in my connected [folder] and draft a weekly report covering completed work, blockers, and next week's priorities. Do not send it.

Sunday family plan

Sunday family plan
